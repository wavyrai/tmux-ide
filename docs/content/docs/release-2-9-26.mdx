`2.9.26` is a CLI/daemon release that makes typing next to busy panes faster,
most of all over SSH. It also lets a client open a stream for one window of a
session, and its installer keeps releases that a running process uses. It adds
no commands. Native Mac alpha 0.1.19 uses the window streams; older apps and
the TUI work with it as before.

## Typing next to busy panes is faster over SSH

When other panes in the session were busy, keys typed over SSH could take a
quarter of a second or more to show, and a burst of short commands well over a
second. Three daemon changes remove most of that wait:

- A pane at tmux's `history-limit` (2000 lines by default, where long agent
  sessions sit) no longer sends its whole history again each time it pauses.
  The daemon's copy of the pane now drops old lines the way tmux does, so it
  stays equal to tmux and nothing has to be captured again.
- A client that is behind on such a pane gets the change (old lines dropped,
  new lines added) instead of the pane's whole history.
- When busy panes have filled the connection, the pane you type into goes
  first. Other panes wait at most 250 ms. On a fast link nothing waits.

Measured on 17 Nov on one Mac: a test daemon with the tmux the app ships,
reached through the Mac app's own SSH tunnel with a 5 ms round trip, typing
into one pane while three others were busy. "Agents" means three panes printing
20 lines a second with full history; "heavy" means three panes redrawing the
whole screen 30 times a second. "5 ms + 50 Mbit" adds a 50 Mbit cap, like a
busy Wi-Fi link. The left column is CLI/daemon 2.9.25 with Mac app 0.1.18; the
right is main on 17 Nov, which has this release's daemon and the Mac app 0.1.19
changes to how keys are sent, but not its trackpad and burst merging. Each
value is the middle of 3 runs in milliseconds, from the key press until the key
is in the app's picture of the screen, with the range across runs in brackets.

| What you do                              | Busy panes | Link           | 2.9.25           | 2.9.26        |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------- | -------------- | ---------------- | ------------- |
| Type one key                             | agents     | 5 ms           | 15 [14–233]      | 13 [12–13]    |
| Type one key                             | heavy      | 5 ms           | 69 [61–123]      | 17 [17–18]    |
| Type one key                             | heavy      | 5 ms + 50 Mbit | 303 [175–388]    | 20 [20–21]    |
| Slowest 1 in 10 keys                     | heavy      | 5 ms + 50 Mbit | 512 [378–682]    | 35 [34–70]    |
| Type a key every 60 ms                   | heavy      | 5 ms           | 111 [99–112]     | 15 [12–20]    |
| 10 short commands (text + Enter) at once | agents     | 5 ms           | 590 [523–681]    | 110 [75–110]  |
| 10 short commands (text + Enter) at once | heavy      | 5 ms + 50 Mbit | 1727 [1681–1996] | 142 [103–152] |

The Mac was busy with other work during these runs, the same for both columns
because the runs alternated. Painting adds at most one screen refresh on top.
They were not run against a real remote machine or with a person typing in the
app. The daemon on the machine that runs tmux does this work, so update remote
machines too.

## Large sessions open one window at a time

A stream could only show a whole session, and the Mac app refuses a session of
more than 24 panes. A client can now open a stream for one window of a session:
the 24-pane limit then applies to that window. Panes of other windows never
enter it. For a client that offers the in-place capability of 2.9.25, the
stream follows its window when another tmux client splits, closes or moves a
pane. Switching windows opens a new stream.

Two read routes gain an opt-in version for this: the scoped inventory with
`?version=2` lists each window and its panes, and the scoped sessions list with
`?version=3` adds the pane count of each session's largest window. Without the
query they answer as before.

Mac app 0.1.19 uses this with a daemon of 2.9.26 or later on the same Mac. See
the [native alpha](https://tmux-ide.com/docs/native-alpha) 0.1.19 notes.

## More connection fixes

- Right after another tmux client changed the panes of a session, the Mac app's
  next stream could close with 1011 `stream-unavailable` because the daemon's
  view of the session had not settled yet. In a soak this happened once in
  each window. The daemon now tries again after 150 ms and 400 ms first.

## Installer

- An update could stop with "Eight retained releases reached" and advise you to
  stop tmux-ide processes and prune. When your tmux server ran from an older
  release, following that advice stopped the server. A release that a running
  process uses no longer counts towards the eight, prune keeps it, and the
  advice no longer says to stop anything. Prune now says, for example, "Removed
  1 retired release; kept 1 in use by a running process".

## Updating

Update with `tmux-ide update` or rerun the installer. A running older daemon
restarts on 2.9.26 by itself; your tmux sessions keep running. Update remote
machines too: the typing fixes are in the daemon on the machine that runs tmux.
